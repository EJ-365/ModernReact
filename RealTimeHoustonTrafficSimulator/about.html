About LTS

Ejay Gabriel

Live Traffic Simulator, or LTS, began with Houston and grew into a living 3D atlas of sixteen U.S. metros. Every city keeps its own roads, airports, weather, local headlines, and airspace while sharing one real-time simulation engine.

Why LTS exists

Cities do not move like static maps. Roads slow, weather changes by neighborhood, flights pass overhead, and local events change the feel of a place. LTS makes that motion explorable in one view.

Houston set the standard: specific roads, fast-changing weather, and a busy sky. The goal is to give every metro that same local character instead of applying a generic city skin.

What I care about

  • Free public data when it makes the city truer
  • Clear labels whenever movement is modeled
  • Road geometry and landmarks that belong to each place
  • Interface detail that supports exploration instead of obscuring the map

Sixteen metros live

Live now: Houston, Austin, San Antonio, Dallas–Fort Worth, Los Angeles, New York, Boston, Chicago, Miami, Seattle, Denver, Atlanta, Phoenix, Philadelphia, Minneapolis–St. Paul, and New Orleans.

Each metro combines researched road geometry, suburb-local weather, traffic context, airports, local news, landmarks, and educational hazard overlays tuned to that place.

Free data, honest context

Created by Ejay Gabriel. LTS uses Three.js and Vite, Open-Meteo weather, Houston TranStar traffic where available, Google News RSS local briefings, OSM road data, and airplanes.live plus other free ADS-B sources.

Live sources can have gaps or rate limits. LTS labels modeled traffic where a public live feed is not available. Hazard modes are educational what-if overlays, never official emergency guidance.
