About the project

Houston Traffic Simulation

A free 3D Houston you can drive, fly and plan trips across, right in your browser. Built by Ejay Gabriel, starting with Houston as the case study: one city done properly before any other city is added.

Why it exists

Maps are static; Houston isn't. Freeways back up, storms roll in from the Gulf, and planes stack up over IAH and Hobby. This project puts all of that into one live 3D view you can move around.

Everything is built from open data, so the roads, buildings and airports are where OpenStreetMap places them. Detail OSM doesn't have (houses in suburbs, windows, trees) is generated, and labelled as modeled where it matters.

What's inside

  • 671 sq mi of Houston: OSM roads, buildings, landmarks, schools, parks and 14 airports
  • Traffic on freeways, ramps, arterials and neighborhood streets, with headlights at night
  • Live weather, clouds, rain and real sunrise and sunset times
  • Live flights with arrival and departure boards
  • Fly-to search with suggestions as you type

Tour Houston

Drive: choose a car, a destination and cruise control from 30 to 100 mph. The ETA follows traffic and speed limits ahead, and you can drive back again when you arrive.

Fly: pick a Cessna 172, an airliner, a helicopter, an F-16 or a C-130, plus your departure and landing airports. Each aircraft has its own cockpit dashboard with live gauges and its own engine sound. Jets raise their landing gear after take-off and lower it again on approach before landing on a real runway.

Explore: click a landmark for directions, a "Fly there" button and its Wikipedia page.

Free data, honest context

Built with Three.js and Vite on OpenStreetMap roads and buildings, Open-Meteo weather, Houston TranStar traffic, Google News RSS headlines, and airplanes.live plus other free ADS-B feeds. No map data is copied from Google Maps, Apple Maps or Street View.

Live feeds can have gaps or rate limits; where there's no live feed, traffic is modeled and labelled that way. Hazard modes are educational what-if overlays, never official emergency guidance.
