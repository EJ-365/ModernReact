Craft

One platform. Sixteen metros.

Built for exploring how cities move: researched freeways, local conditions, and a shared 3D engine tuned to each place.

Real freeway geometry

Elevated decks, congestion ribbons, and corridors built from researched polylines — I-10 in Houston, MoPac in Austin, FDR in New York.

Suburb-local weather

Every district gets its own Open-Meteo sample. Rain at a world point comes from the nearest suburb only — never borrowed from downtown or a far wet cell.

ADS-B that keeps flying

Live aircraft over IAH, AUS, DFW, LAX, JFK, and more. Parked gate clutter at low ground speed is filtered so the sky stays in motion.

Hazard what-ifs

Hurricane, wildfire, flood corridors, and educational nuclear drill rings — overlays, not official alerts.

Landmarks & places

Physical attractions per metro — Capitol, Statue of Liberty, Reunion Tower — plus hover cards and Wikipedia deep dives.

FAQ

Built to stay useful.

What is live, what is modeled, and where the information comes from.

Is Live Traffic Simulator free?

Yes. The simulator uses public and free-to-access sources, with no paid traffic, weather, airport, or news service required to use the experience.

Where do the local headlines come from?

Headlines are retrieved from Google News RSS with a query for the active metro. Selecting a headline opens the original publisher’s reporting.

How are flights and airport boards powered?

Aircraft positions come from free ADS-B sources. Flight cards and airport board rows can open the matching aircraft or callsign on airplanes.live for further tracking.

Are all traffic speeds live?

Houston uses live TranStar data where it is available. Other metros and uncovered corridors use clearly labeled modeled traffic, which keeps the map active without presenting estimates as measured conditions.

What is modeled on the map?

Road geometry, districts, airports, and landmarks are researched per city. Weather, alerts, local news, and flight positions update from their respective live or public feeds.
